The Option Buyer's Field Guide

A month-long, no-fluff path from "what is a call" to a written, tested intraday option-buying playbook — with the exact filters that stop you from buying on the wrong day.

How to use this

Read one day. Do the drill. Don't skip.

Each day is a concept, why it matters specifically to a buyer (long premium), and a drill you can finish in 20–40 minutes. The first two weeks are paper only. You are not allowed to risk real money until Day 20 — and only then in tiny size.

The rule of the month: observe first, trade last. Screen-time on live price + option chain beats any book. Log everything from Day 1.
The trap this guide kills: "I was right on direction but still lost money." That sentence is the whole reason options buyers bleed. By Day 19 you'll know exactly why.

The buyer's edge — and its price

When you buy an option you have defined risk (max loss = premium paid) and unlimited/​large upside. That asymmetry is the attraction. The price you pay for it:

  • Time decay (theta) works against you every single day — you are renting the position.
  • You need direction + speed + size of move all correct, not just direction.
  • Most out-of-the-money options expire worthless. The seller collecting your premium knows this.

So a buyer's whole job is to only pay rent when the building is about to move — enter when a real move is likely and soon, and refuse to buy on quiet, choppy, decaying days. Everything below builds toward being able to tell those two situations apart, mechanically.

1

Foundations

Days 1–6 · what you're actually buying and how it pays off

DAY 01

What an option really is

Concept

An option is a contract giving the buyer the right, not the obligation, to buy (call) or sell (put) an underlying at a fixed strike price before/at expiry. The buyer pays a premium; the seller (writer) receives it and takes on the obligation. In India you trade index options (NIFTY, BANKNIFTY, FINNIFTY, SENSEX) in fixed lots, with weekly and monthly expiries, cash-settled.

Why it matters to a buyer

You are not buying the index. You are buying a time-limited, leveraged bet on movement. Leverage cuts both ways: a small premium controls a large notional, so percentage swings on the premium are violent.

Drill

Open your broker's option chain for the nearest weekly NIFTY expiry. Note the lot size, the ATM strike, and the premium of the ATM call and put. Write down: "If I buy 1 lot of the ATM call, my max loss in ₹ is ___."

Rule: before every trade you must be able to say your max loss in rupees. For a buyer it's always premium × lot size × lots.
DAY 02

Calls, puts & the payoff diagram

Concept

A call gains when the underlying rises above strike; a put gains when it falls below strike. At expiry the payoff is a "hockey stick": flat (you lose only premium) until the strike, then linear.

Why it matters to a buyer

Your break-even is not the strike — it's strike ± premium. The market must move past your break-even before expiry for you to make money. That gap is the seller's cushion.

Drill

For today's ATM call, compute the break-even. Then check how many points the index must move (and by when) to reach it. Ask: "Does the index normally move that much in the time I plan to hold?"

DAY 03

Moneyness: ITM, ATM, OTM

Concept

ITM (in-the-money) has real exercise value; ATM sits at the current price; OTM is pure hope — all time value. Deep OTM options are cheap because they probably expire worthless.

Why it matters to a buyer

Cheap ≠ good. OTM options have low delta (barely respond to the move) and decay fastest in % terms. Slightly ITM / ATM options track the underlying better and are the workhorse for directional buyers.

Trap: the lure of "₹5 options that can become ₹50." They usually become ₹0. Lottery tickets are a strategy only with tiny, pre-defined lottery money.

Drill

Line up three strikes (one ITM, ATM, one OTM). Watch them for 30 minutes live. Note how much each moves for the same 20-point index move. You are watching delta before we name it.

DAY 04

Premium anatomy: intrinsic + time value

Concept

Premium = intrinsic value (how deep ITM it is, ≥0) + extrinsic / time value (everything else: time left × volatility). ATM options are almost entirely time value.

Why it matters to a buyer

When you buy an OTM/ATM option you are buying a pile of time value that decays to zero. If the move doesn't come, the melt is guaranteed. You are long time value and it is a wasting asset.

Drill

For an ITM, ATM and OTM strike, split each premium into intrinsic + time value. See how ATM is ~100% time value.

DAY 05

Expiry mechanics & the settlement trap

Concept

Weekly options expire fast — a Tuesday/Thursday-expiry weekly loses time value hour by hour, and on expiry day the decay is near-vertical. Index options are cash-settled at the expiry-day average.

Why it matters to a buyer

Expiry-day ATM buying is a pure gamma-vs-theta fight: huge, fast swings but brutal decay if price stalls. It is the most seductive and most dangerous playground for a beginner.

STT / holding-to-expiry trap: letting an ITM option expire can trigger higher settlement costs and slippage vs simply selling it before close. As a buyer, square off; don't "let it settle."

Drill

On the next expiry day, paper trade only. Track one ATM option's premium every 15 minutes from open to close. Watch how it bleeds when the index goes sideways. Feel the theta.

DAY 06

The buyer's core problem

Concept

To profit as a buyer you must be right on direction, magnitude, and timing — all three. A seller only needs the move to not happen. That's why sellers win more often but buyers win bigger.

Why it matters

Your edge is not "predicting more often." It's asymmetry + selectivity: small, frequent, capped losses punctuated by a few large wins on days you correctly identified as explosive. Skipping bad days is the strategy.

Rule: a great option buyer trades rarely and sizes the winners. If you're trading every hour, you're the seller's income.

Drill

Review your paper log so far. Mark each hypothetical trade as win/loss and write the single reason. Patterns start here.

2

The Greeks

Days 7–13 · the four forces moving your premium

DAY 07

Delta — your directional exposure

Concept

Delta = how much the premium moves per 1-point move in the underlying (0→1 for calls, 0→−1 for puts). ATM ≈ 0.5. It doubles as a rough probability of expiring ITM.

Why it matters to a buyer

Low-delta OTM options barely move on a real rally — you're "right" and still flat. For trend rides, buyers often pick delta 0.5–0.65 so the option actually participates.

Drill

Find each strike's delta in your chain. Predict the premium change for a 30-point move, then watch and check.

DAY 08

Gamma — the accelerator

Concept

Gamma = how fast delta changes. It's highest at-the-money and near expiry. High gamma means a small move can rocket a near-worthless option — and reverse just as fast.

Why it matters

Gamma is the buyer's friend on a fast move and enemy in chop (whipsaws your delta). It's why expiry-day ATM trades feel like a coin-flip on steroids.

Drill

On expiry vs a far expiry, compare ATM gamma. Note how the near-expiry premium reacts far more violently to the same move.

DAY 09

Theta — the rent you pay

Concept

Theta = premium lost per day just from time passing. It is negative for buyers and accelerates as expiry approaches, fastest for ATM options.

Why it matters

Every minute a trade sits still, theta takes money. This is why buyers need the move to come soon, and why a time-stop ("if it hasn't worked in N minutes, exit") is a core buyer tool.

Rule: direction can be right and theta still wins if you're early. Enter close to the trigger, not in anticipation.

Drill

Track an ATM option on a flat day for one hour. Quantify the ₹ lost to pure decay. That number is your daily rent.

DAY 10

Vega — your volatility exposure

Concept

Vega = premium change per 1% change in implied volatility (IV). Buyers are long vega: rising IV inflates your option, falling IV deflates it — independent of direction.

Why it matters

You can be right on direction and lose because IV dropped (vega loss). The ideal buy is low IV that then expands. The nightmare is buying high IV that collapses (next day's topic).

Drill

Note the ATM IV now. After a sharp index move, note it again. See how IV (and your premium) jumps on fear/expansion.

DAY 11

When the Greeks gang up on you

Concept

The Greeks act together. Expiry-day ATM: huge gamma (explosive) fights huge theta (brutal decay). A pre-event OTM call: you might win on direction (delta) but lose on IV crush (vega) after the event.

Drill

For each of your paper trades, tag which Greek helped and which hurt. This single habit rewires how you pick trades.

DAY 12

Picking a strike, using the Greeks

Concept

Match the strike to the plan: trend ride → slightly ITM (delta 0.55–0.65, less theta/IV dependence); fast scalp near a level → ATM (max gamma); defined lottery on a breakout → 1 strike OTM, tiny size.

Heuristic: the further OTM you go, the more you need a big and fast move. Beginners systematically buy too far OTM because it's "cheap."

Drill

Write a rule: "For an intraday trend trade I will buy the ___ strike (delta ~___), risking ₹___." Commit it to your plan.

DAY 13

Greeks review — the position checklist

Concept

Before any buy, run the mental checklist: Delta (will it participate?), Gamma (how twitchy near expiry?), Theta (what's my daily rent / time-stop?), Vega (is IV cheap or about to crush?).

Drill

Build a one-line pre-trade card with the four Greeks. Use it on 5 paper trades today. If any Greek is clearly against you, skip.

Milestone: you now understand why "right on direction, still lost" happens. Phase 3 makes it precise.
3

Volatility

Days 14–19 · the single biggest edge most buyers ignore

DAY 14

Implied Volatility (IV)

Concept

IV is the market's expected future volatility, baked into the premium. High IV = expensive options; low IV = cheap. IV rises on fear/uncertainty and falls on calm/after events.

Why it matters to a buyer

As a buyer, IV is the price tag on your bet. Buying high-IV options means you overpaid and need an even bigger move just to break even. IV, not just direction, decides your win rate.

Drill

Note ATM IV for NIFTY and BANKNIFTY. Compare — BANKNIFTY usually runs higher. Understand "high" is relative to each instrument.

DAY 15

IV Percentile / IV Rank — the context

Concept

Raw IV is meaningless alone. IV Percentile (IVP) / IV Rank (IVR) tell you where today's IV sits versus its own past year: is volatility cheap or rich for this instrument?

Drill

If your platform shows IVP/IVR, record it daily for a week. If not, note this is exactly what a rolling IV history (like the one your own dashboard can store) would compute.

DAY 16

IV crush — the silent killer

Concept

Before known events (results, RBI policy, budget, big data) IV inflates. Once the event passes, IV collapses and premiums deflate — often instantly. This is IV crush.

Classic beginner loss: buy a call before the event, event goes your way, and the option still loses money because IV crushed harder than the move helped. Direction right, trade wrong.

Why it matters

Never buy elevated IV into an event unless your explicit thesis is a move bigger than the implied expected move. Usually, the event is a reason not to be long premium.

Drill

Find the next scheduled event. Track ATM IV before and right after. Watch the crush. Never forget it.

DAY 17

Expected move & the ATM straddle

Concept

The ATM straddle price (ATM call + ATM put) ≈ the move the market is pricing in for the period. That's your expected move. If your profit target is smaller than the expected move already priced, you're paying up front for a move you need to beat.

Drill

Compute today's expected move from the ATM straddle. Compare with the index's typical daily range. Are your targets realistic?

DAY 18

Realized vs implied volatility

Concept

Realized volatility is how much the underlying actually moved. If realized keeps coming in below implied, sellers are winning and buyers are systematically overpaying.

Why it matters

A simple, powerful gauge: track a hypothetical long ATM straddle intraday. If it's steadily bleeding, realized < implied — a theta day, hostile to buyers. (This is exactly the logic in your Buyer's Day Meter, Day 25.)

Drill

For 3 days, log the ATM straddle at open and close. Note days it expanded (buyer's day) vs decayed (seller's day).

DAY 19

Volatility regime: expansion vs contraction

Concept

Markets alternate between contraction (coiling, low range, chop) and expansion (trending, wide range). Buyers make money in expansion; they donate it in contraction.

Rule: your job is to buy at the start of expansion (low IV, tight range breaking out), not in the middle of a calm range.

Drill

Mark the last 10 sessions as "expansion" or "contraction" from the daily chart. Notice they cluster — regimes persist.

Milestone: you can now judge whether options are cheap/rich and whether the day favors buyers before picking a direction.

Reading the tape

Days 20–25 · turning price + option-chain flow into a decision

DAY 20

VWAP — intraday fair value

First allowed live trade day — micro size only.

Concept

VWAP (volume-weighted average price) is the intraday "fair value." Price above & holding VWAP = buyers in control (favor CE); below = sellers (favor PE). Price repeatedly crossing VWAP = chop.

Drill

Watch the index vs VWAP for a full session. Count the crosses. Few crosses + one side = tradeable. Many = stand aside.

DAY 21

OI + premium flow read together

Concept

Why it matters

On a rally, put writing (PE OI up + PE premium down) is bullish, not bearish — a classic misread that flips beginners the wrong way. Always pair OI with premium.

Drill

Classify the ATM CE and PE into one quadrant every 15 min for a session. Correlate with the index direction.

DAY 22

PCR & OI skew — positioning context

Concept

PCR (put/call ratio) and OI skew show where crowd positioning sits. Extremes can mark exhaustion. Use as context, not a trigger — they're noisy intraday and easily misread.

Trap: trading PCR alone. "High PCR = bearish" fails constantly because heavy put OI can be put writing (bullish). Context only.

Drill

Note PCR at open and midday. See how loosely it maps to direction. Demote it to a supporting actor.

DAY 23

Levels: support, resistance, hourly zones

Concept

Enter at levels, not in the middle of nowhere. Prior-day high/low, opening range, and hourly-timeframe support/resistance give you a tight stop and a clear invalidation.

Why it matters to a buyer

Buying at a level means a small adverse move invalidates you cheaply (small premium loss) while a hold gives a large run. That's the asymmetry you need — good reward:risk.

Drill

Mark hourly S/R before the open. Only allow yourself to buy CE near support (with reversal up) or on a resistance break; PE mirror.

DAY 24

Confluence — stacking the odds

Concept

No single signal is enough. Confluence = multiple independent checks agreeing. Your own engine uses 10: spot vs open+VWAP, futures vs spot, ATM premium, OI skew, call activity, put activity, skew direction, day bias, at hourly support, at hourly resistance. 7+ aligned → a CE or PE direction.

Your dashboard already does this

The Option Long Confluence card scores CE vs PE across the 10 checks and only prints a direction when 7+ align and the spot-trend gate agrees. Treat <7 as "no trade." CE PE

Drill

For every potential trade, count how many checks agree. If under 7, you are forbidden to enter. Log the count vs outcome.

DAY 25

Regime filter + riding the runners

Concept

Even perfect confluence loses on a chop/theta day. Filter the day first, then the trade. Two tools you already have:

Buyer's Day Meter

Scores the session 0–100 from range, trend efficiency, VWAP crosses, ADX, and straddle decay/momentum. AVOID = stand aside or scalp only; BUYER-FRIENDLY = movement is beating theta.

Sector RS vs NIFTY

Ranks sectors against the index and surfaces the strongest runners and weakest laggards. On index-option days it tells you whether leadership is broad (trend fuel) or absent (chop).

Rule: Day filter → direction (confluence) → level → strike (Greeks) → size → exit plan. In that order, every time.

Drill

Before trading, read the Buyer's Day Meter. If AVOID, you may only paper trade today. Record how AVOID days would have treated you.

5

Trader craft

Days 26–30 · the boring skills that actually keep the money

DAY 26

Risk per trade & position sizing

Concept

Decide risk first, size second. Risk a fixed small % of capital per trade (e.g. 0.5–1%) and set a hard daily loss limit (e.g. 2–3%). Since a bought option's max loss is the premium, sizing is simple and exact.

Trap: "high-conviction" oversizing. One oversized loss erases ten disciplined wins. The market punishes ego, not analysis.

Drill

Compute your per-trade ₹ risk and max lots for a 1% rule. Write your daily loss limit. Automate the stop mentally: hit it → done for the day.

DAY 27

Exits: the hard part

Concept

Entries are glamorous; exits pay you. Have three exits ready: target (or trail a runner), stop (level invalidated), and time-stop (theta-aware: if it hasn't worked in N minutes, leave).

Rule: never average down a losing long option. You're adding theta and vega risk to a thesis the market is rejecting.

Why time-stops matter to buyers

Unlike a seller, sitting in a stalled long bleeds you. If the expected move hasn't started promptly, the trade thesis (a soon move) is already wrong.

Drill

Predefine all three exits before entry on every paper/live trade. Log which exit fired and whether you honored it.

DAY 28

Concept

You can't improve what you don't measure. Log every trade: setup, confluence count, Buyer's Day score, strike/Greeks, entry/exit, ₹ risked, result, and one honest note on execution vs plan.

DAY 29

Psychology & tilt control

Concept

The edge lives or dies on behavior. The four horsemen: FOMO (chasing), revenge (after a loss), overtrading (boredom), oversizing (greed). Rules exist to survive the moments emotion takes over.

Rule set that survives emotion: max N trades/day · hard daily loss limit · no trade under 7/10 confluence · no trade on AVOID days · walk away after the limit.

Drill

Write your personal "tilt triggers" and the exact rule that neutralizes each. Put it where you'll see it while trading.

DAY 30

Your written playbook

Concept

Graduation = a one-page written plan you actually follow. It removes decisions from the heat of the moment.

The daily routine (put it on your wall)

Pre-market: mark hourly S/R, note events (IV-crush risk), check overnight bias.
Open: read the Buyer's Day Meter → AVOID means paper/scalp only.
Setup: wait for 7+ confluence at a level; confirm sector/breadth (Sector RS).
Execute: pick strike by Greeks, size by 1% risk, set target + stop + time-stop.
Manage: honor exits, never average down, stop at daily loss limit.
Post-market: journal every trade + one improvement note.

Milestone: you now have a complete, filtered, risk-bounded process — and the tools in your own dashboard to run it daily.

Final drill

Write your one-page playbook. Commit to 20 sessions following it with micro size, journaling every trade, before scaling up.

The 10 laws of the option buyer

  1. Filter the day before the trade. Chop days are unwinnable; skipping them is the edge.
  2. Right direction isn't enough — IV, timing and size decide the outcome.
  3. Buy cheap volatility, sell expensive — respect IVP; fear IV crush.
  4. Theta is rent. Enter at the trigger, not in anticipation; use a time-stop.
  5. Read OI with premium, never alone. Put writing is bullish.
  6. Enter at levels for tight stops and big reward:risk.
  7. 7+ confluence or no trade. Count, don't feel.
  8. Risk 1%, cap the day. Survival first; ruin is a sizing problem.
  9. Exits pay. Never average a losing long.
  10. Journal or you're gambling. Measured edge compounds; unmeasured hope doesn't.
Reality check (read this every week). This is an educational study guide, not investment advice. Index option buying is high-risk; SEBI studies show the large majority of individual F&O traders lose money, and buyers face the added drag of time decay and IV crush. Nothing here guarantees profit. Trade only risk capital, start on paper, size tiny when live, and treat the survival rules (daily loss limit, 1% risk, no-trade filters) as non-negotiable. The tools referenced (Buyer's Day Meter, Confluence, Sector RS) are decision aids, not crystal balls — they exist to keep you out of bad trades, not to promise good ones.
