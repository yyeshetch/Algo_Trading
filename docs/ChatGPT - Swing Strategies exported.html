Professional Swing Trading Playbook

A systematic framework for finding market leaders using momentum, relative strength, trend following, earnings acceleration, institutional participation, volatility contraction, breakouts, pullbacks and disciplined risk management.

Important: No trading strategy guarantees profits. Historical evidence supports certain factors and methodologies, but future performance is uncertain. Momentum and trend strategies can experience severe drawdowns and execution can be materially different from backtests.

2. Relative-Strength Momentum

The core question is not: "Is this stock cheap?"

Instead: "Is this stock materially stronger than most of the stocks I could buy?"

Example Screening Criteria

  • Price above ₹100
  • Average daily traded value above ₹5–10 crore
  • 20-day return positive
  • 3-month return in the top 20–30%
  • 6-month return in the top 20–30%
  • 12-month return in the top 20–30%
  • Price above 50 DMA
  • Price above 200 DMA
  • 50 DMA above 200 DMA
  • Relative strength versus Nifty improving
  • Relative strength versus sector improving
  • Volume increasing on advances

The purpose is to create a leader universe, not to blindly buy every stock that passes.

3. Breakout From a Proper Base

A high-quality breakout often follows: 

Strong Trend
     ↓
Consolidation
     ↓
Volatility Contracts
     ↓
Supply Dries Up
     ↓
Breakout
     ↓
Volume / Price Expansion

Examples include:

  • Cup and handle
  • Flat base
  • Volatility contraction pattern
  • Tight flag
  • Rectangle
  • Ascending base
  • Reaccumulation
  • IPO base

Ideal Setup

  1. Strong prior uptrend.
  2. Stock consolidates rather than collapses.
  3. Corrections become smaller.
  4. Volume dries up during contraction.
  5. Price remains above major moving averages.
  6. Relative strength remains strong.
  7. Sector is healthy.
  8. Price approaches resistance.
  9. Breakout occurs with meaningful volume.
  10. Price closes strongly.

4. Earnings + Momentum

One of the strongest combinations is:

Fundamental Acceleration + Price Acceleration

  • EPS growth accelerating
  • Revenue growth accelerating
  • Margin expansion
  • Positive earnings surprise
  • Positive guidance
  • Increasing institutional interest
  • Strong relative strength
  • Breakout following earnings

A good company alone isn't necessarily a good swing trade. The preferred combination is:

Improving fundamentals + market recognition + technical confirmation

7. Stage Analysis

Stage 1 Base / accumulation
Stage 2 Advancing trend
Stage 3 Distribution / maturity
Stage 4 Decline

Swing traders should generally concentrate on stocks entering or already in Stage 2, rather than attempting to turn a Stage 4 stock into a Stage 2 stock because it looks cheap.

8. Trend Following / Turtle Breakout

The core concept associated with the Turtle experiment is:

Buy Strength. Sell Weakness. Let Winners Run. Cut Losers.

Generic System

Entry:
Close > Highest Close of Previous N Sessions

Exit:
Close < Lowest Close of Previous M Sessions

Position:
Volatility-adjusted sizing

The precise parameters should be tested rather than treated as magical constants.

13. Mean Reversion

Mean reversion can work, but I would place it below momentum/trend strategies for a growth-stock swing framework.

Higher-Quality Mean Reversion

  • Strong longer-term trend
  • Short-term sharp selloff
  • Oversold conditions
  • Support nearby
  • Selling-volume exhaustion
  • Market/sector not collapsing
  • Reversal trigger
Do not confuse cheapness with mean reversion. A stock falling 70% does not automatically become a good mean-reversion trade.

23. Position Sizing

Position size should be determined by the amount you're willing to lose, not by how much capital you have available.

Example

Account = ₹10,00,000

Risk per trade = 0.5% = ₹5,000

Entry = ₹1,000

Stop = ₹950

Risk/share = ₹50

Position size = ₹5,000 / ₹50 = 100 shares

Position value = ₹1,00,000

You're therefore risking approximately 0.5% of the account, rather than simply risking the entire ₹1 lakh position.

Account for slippage and gap risk. A stop price does not necessarily guarantee execution at that exact price, particularly in fast-moving or illiquid markets.

24. Exit System

Exit 1 — Thesis Failure

Price breaks the level that invalidates the setup.

Exit 2 — Failed Breakout

Large volume reversal and close back into the base can indicate failure.

Exit 3 — Relative-Strength Deterioration

If the market remains strong while the stock becomes materially weaker, treat it as a warning.

Exit 4 — Trend Violation

Repeated closes below important structural support may justify reducing or exiting the position.

Exit 5 — Trailing Stop

  • Swing lows
  • ATR
  • Moving averages
  • Previous breakout levels

Exit 6 — Climactic Move

Parabolic acceleration combined with extreme volume and extension can be a reason to begin taking profits or tightening the trailing mechanism.

25. Expectancy

Don't judge a strategy solely by its win rate. 

Expectancy =
(Win Rate × Average Win)
−
(Loss Rate × Average Loss)

Example:

Win rate = 45%

Average winner = +4R

Loss rate = 55%

Average loser = −1R

Expectancy = +1.25R

A strategy can therefore be profitable even if it loses more trades than it wins, provided its winners are sufficiently larger than its losers.

30. Weekly Trading Workflow

Saturday / Sunday

  1. Determine market regime.
  2. Rank sectors.
  3. Run stock scans.
  4. Check fundamental acceleration.
  5. Check institutional participation.
  6. Manually review charts.
  7. Reduce the universe to 5–15 candidates.
  8. Prepare entry/stop/position-size plans.
  9. Set alerts.

During the Week

  1. Do not chase extended moves.
  2. Wait for the predefined trigger.
  3. Calculate position size before entering.
  4. Place risk controls.
  5. Track relative strength.
  6. Manage winners according to predefined rules.
  7. Record every trade.

End of Week

  • Review every trade.
  • Review missed setups.
  • Measure expectancy.
  • Measure slippage.
  • Identify rule violations.
  • Update the research database.

32. The Core Principle

Don't search for stocks that merely might go up.

Search for stocks that are:

Already demonstrating unusual strength, in strong industries, with improving fundamentals, under constructive supply/demand conditions, approaching a technically favorable point where downside can be defined tightly.

The scanner finds candidates. The chart finds the setup. The risk model determines whether you can survive. The journal determines whether you actually have an edge.

33. Four-Week Implementation Plan

Week 1 Build the market, sector and relative-strength framework.
Week 2 Build the five scanners.
Week 3 Backtest/replay 100–200 historical setups.
Week 4 Paper trade the exact rules.

After that, use very small live size, collect at least 50–100 trades, measure the actual expectancy, and only then consider scaling.
